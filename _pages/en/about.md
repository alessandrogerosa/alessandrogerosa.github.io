I'm an assistant professor in `cultural sociology` at the department of social and political science at the University of Milan.

My research adopts a cultural and critical perspective to analyse how humans seek meaningful and authentic lives in late modern capitalism, through `working` and `consumption` practices.

I'm the author of [The Hipster Economy: Taste and Authenticity in Late Modern Capitalism](https://www.uclpress.co.uk/products/230904), published in Open Access by UCL Press (the book's pdf is freely available at the provided link). You can access all the articles and podcasts discussing my book in the dedicated page.

My research projects have focused on `neo-craft` consumption and work, on `digital cultures` and on `urban transformation` processes. Currently, I'm researching `politicised` forms of neo-craft work and consumption, analysing in particular
1. political interconnections and organising among artisans and other actors
2. the management of political boundaries and tensions between political and economic goals
3. the existing and potential future relationships between urban and rural geographies.


\
&nbsp;

Other recent research interests of mine include:
* the impact of digital media, platforms and GenAI on the mental and physical wellbeing of individuals, in particular of those who are victims of structural discriminations
* counter-datafication projects
* the relevance of Sartre's thought for sociological research
* marxist and heterodox theorists in evolutionary biology (eg., Gould, Lewontin, Levins) and their relevance for debates in the social sciences.
